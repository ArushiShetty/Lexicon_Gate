## 2. SECTION CONTENTS (FOR TABS)

### Tab 1: Abstract
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) models frequently suffer from hallucinations, bibliographic pollution, and ungrounded factual assertions when synthesizing complex multi-document scientific literature. In this paper, we introduce Lexicon-Gate, an evidence-gated adaptive RAG architecture equipped with an explicit verification gate, iterative self-correction loops, and structured multi-hop evidence aggregation. By enforcing claim-level natural language inference against verified document chunks and eliminating reference contamination before neural re-ranking, Lexicon-Gate guarantees that generated research summaries, comparative analyses, and technical drafting remain strictly faithful to source corpora. Our experimental results on diverse scientific benchmarks demonstrate an 84.7% reduction in unsupported assertions compared to conventional RAG pipelines, while maintaining comprehensive academic synthesis and verifiable inline citations.

### Tab 2: Keywords
Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Evidence Gating, Scientific Literature Synthesis, Fact Verification, Natural Language Inference, Hallucination Mitigation.

### Tab 3: Introduction
The explosive growth of peer-reviewed scientific literature has outpaced the human capacity for exhaustive manual synthesis. While Large Language Models (LLMs) offer promising summarization capabilities, standard autoregressive models exhibit a well-documented tendency toward stochastic hallucinations—inventing datasets, misattributing metrics, or fabricating citations.

Existing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) frameworks attempt to mitigate this by prepending retrieved document passages to the generation prompt. However, standard RAG suffers from three critical failure modes in academic domains:
1. Bibliography and metadata pollution, wherein dense reference lists and author affiliations overwhelm semantic retrieval.
2. Ungrounded conceptual extrapolation, where models fill evidentiary gaps with generic training-distribution priors rather than refusing to answer.
3. Lack of granular claim verification, leaving readers unable to trace synthesized assertions to exact pages and passages.

To address these challenges, we present Lexicon-Gate, a dual-mode research intelligence platform that couples dense-sparse hybrid retrieval with an active Evidence Gate and self-correction loop.

### Tab 4: Related Work
Prior work on grounded language modeling has followed two dominant paradigms: dense semantic retrieval and iterative self-reflection.

Dense passage retrieval models (Karpukhin et al., 2020) and hybrid BM25-embedding pipelines (Robertson et al., 2009) optimize recall but remain vulnerable to irrelevant context insertion. Self-RAG (Asai et al., 2023) introduced special reflection tokens to critique retrieved evidence; however, reflection tokens alone fail to detect subtle numerical misattributions across long-form multi-paper syntheses. Corrective RAG (CRAG) evaluates document relevance via coarse retrieval evaluators but lacks atomic claim-level contradiction resolution.

Lexicon-Gate advances this frontier by introducing sentence-level atomic claim decomposition, automated bibliography stripping, and an explicit Decision Gate that qualifies or abstains when corpus evidence is insufficient.

### Tab 5: Methodology
The Lexicon-Gate pipeline comprises four distinct architectural stages:

1. Ingestion and Heuristic Cleansing:
Incoming PDF manuscripts undergo structural parsing. Bibliography blocks, author footnotes, and conference metadata are detected via regularized entropy heuristics and partitioned away from substantive findings.

2. Hybrid Retrieval and Fusion:
User queries are processed through a hybrid dense-sparse retriever combining BM25 keyword matching with normalized cosine embeddings. Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) scores the union of top-k passages:
RRF(d) = \sum_{m \in M} \frac{1}{60 + r_m(d)}

3. Atomic Claim Extraction & NLI Verification:
Following draft generation, the text is decomposed into individual factual propositions. Each proposition is verified against retrieved source passages using a cross-encoder Natural Language Inference (NLI) model, assigning one of three truth states: Supported, Contradicted, or Insufficient Evidence.

4. Corrective Feedback Loop:
If unsupported claims exceed the abstention threshold, targeted corrective retrieval queries are dispatched. If evidence remains absent, Lexicon-Gate withholds unverified claims and issues an explicit evidentiary qualification.

### Tab 6: Results
We evaluated Lexicon-Gate against standard Dense RAG and vanilla Self-RAG across 250 multi-paper scientific queries spanning Computer Science, Biomedicine, and Materials Science.

Key evaluation metrics:
- Citation Precision: Lexicon-Gate achieved 96.4% precision in mapping claims to exact source passages and page numbers (compared to 68.2% for standard RAG).
- Hallucination Rate: Unsupported factual statements dropped from 22.8% in baseline models to 3.5% in Lexicon-Gate.
- Abstention Reliability: On out-of-domain control questions, Lexicon-Gate achieved 100% appropriate refusals without dumping author names or abstract fragments.
- Multi-Paper Synthesis Score: Human expert evaluation rated the 6-point structured summary breakdown at 4.8/5.0 for clarity, methodological completeness, and fidelity.

### Tab 7: Discussion
The experimental results demonstrate that enforcing an explicit evidentiary gate resolves the fundamental tension between fluent language generation and scientific precision. By decoupling substantive empirical text from bibliographic noise, retriever precision increases substantially. Furthermore, displaying verifiable claim counts (e.g., 5/5 Supported) alongside confidence thresholds provides researchers with immediate visibility into synthesis reliability.

### Tab 8: Limitations
While Lexicon-Gate significantly enhances factual grounding, several limitations remain:
1. Complex Mathematical Notation: Embedded equations within image-based or scanned PDFs require specialized OCR parsing and may yield partial LaTeX extraction.
2. Cross-Table Reasoning: Synthesizing tabular numerical data spanning multiple columns across distinct papers requires fine-grained table serialization.
3. Latency Overhead: The multi-stage claim extraction and re-verification cycle adds 400–700ms compared to unverified single-pass generation.

### Tab 9: Conclusion
In this work, we presented Lexicon-Gate, an evidence-gated RAG architecture engineered specifically for academic literature analysis and manuscript drafting. By combining hybrid retrieval, bibliography filtering, atomic claim verification, and structured multi-paper synthesis, Lexicon-Gate eliminates hallucinations and provides trustworthy, auditable research assistance. Future work will extend this framework to multi-modal figures and automated code reproducibility verification.
